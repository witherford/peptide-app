What Are Peptides?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids — the same building blocks that make up proteins. Your body produces thousands of peptides naturally; they act as signalling molecules, hormones, neurotransmitters, and growth factors. Insulin, GLP-1, and oxytocin are all peptides your body already makes.

Research peptides are either identical to these natural compounds or slightly modified versions designed to be more stable, potent, or targeted. Because they are made of amino acids, they are broken down by the digestive system — which is why most must be injected rather than taken orally.

🔑 Key concept: Peptides work by binding to specific receptors and telling your cells to do something they already know how to do — just more of it. They are not foreign chemicals; they speak your body's own language.

What Equipment Do You Need?
1
Peptide vial — lyophilised (freeze-dried) powder, usually 2mg, 5mg, or 10mg. Arrives as a white/off-white powder at the bottom of a sealed glass vial.
2
Bacteriostatic water (bac water) — sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol. The benzyl alcohol prevents bacterial growth, keeping your reconstituted peptide stable for 4–6 weeks in the fridge. Do NOT use plain saline or tap water.
3
U-100 insulin syringes — 29–31 gauge, 0.5ml or 1ml. The fine needle makes subcutaneous injection virtually painless. These are the standard tool for all peptide injections.

How to Reconstitute a Peptide

Reconstitution means dissolving the dry peptide powder in bacteriostatic water to create an injectable solution. The amount of water you add determines the concentration — and therefore how many units to draw per dose.

1
Wipe the top of both the peptide vial and the bac water vial with alcohol swabs. Let them dry for 10 seconds.
2
Draw the desired amount of bac water into your syringe. 2ml is the standard starting point — it gives simple, clean numbers on a U-100 syringe.
3
Inject the bac water into the peptide vial by aiming the stream at the glass wall, not directly onto the powder. This prevents foaming which can denature the peptide.
4
Gently swirl the vial (do not shake) until the powder is fully dissolved. The solution should be clear.
5
Store immediately in the refrigerator at 2–8°C. Label with the date. Use within 4–6 weeks.
💡 Standard ratio to remember: 5mg vial + 2ml water = 2,500 mcg/ml concentration. A 250 mcg dose = 10 units on your syringe. Use the Reconstitution Calculator above for any other combination.
Best Peptides to Start With

As a beginner, prioritise peptides with the strongest safety records, most published research, and fewest side effects. The list below is ordered by typical beginner suitability.

BPC-157 — Healing & gut repair
The most popular beginner peptide. Exceptional safety record, no reported toxicity at research doses, and versatile — helps tendons, ligaments, gut, and muscle. 250–500 mcg daily SC or oral. A great first peptide for anyone dealing with an injury.
TB-500 — Systemic repair & flexibility
Works systemically — you don't need to inject near the injury. Reduces scar tissue and improves range of motion. Often stacked with BPC-157 for a powerful healing protocol. 2–5 mg twice weekly for 4–8 weeks.
Ipamorelin — GH pulse & sleep
The cleanest GH secretagogue — no meaningful cortisol or prolactin increase. Taken at night before bed, it improves deep sleep and produces a natural GH pulse. 100–200 mcg SC before bed. Ideal for anyone wanting GH benefits without the complexity of exogenous GH.
Selank — Anxiety & stress
Intranasal — no injections needed. Gentle anxiolytic effect without sedation or dependence. Good entry point for anyone curious about peptides for mental health or stress. 250 mcg intranasally once or twice daily.
GHK-Cu — Collagen & skin
Can be used topically (no injection required) for skin, hair, and collagen support. One of the best-studied peptides for topical application. 1–2 mg/day SC or topical. Very well tolerated with an excellent safety profile.
Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid
✗
Using plain saline instead of bac water — plain saline has no preservative. Your reconstituted peptide will only last 24 hours and risks contamination. Always use bacteriostatic water.
✗
Shaking the vial to mix — vigorous shaking creates foam and can break the peptide's molecular structure. Always swirl gently.
✗
Leaving reconstituted peptide at room temperature — once mixed, it must go straight in the fridge. Peptides in solution degrade rapidly at room temperature.
✗
Starting too many peptides at once — begin with one or two peptides so you can clearly attribute any effect (positive or negative) to the right compound.
✗
Ignoring injection site rotation — always rotate between sites (left abdomen, right abdomen, thigh, flank) to avoid scar tissue build-up and maintain absorption.
✗
Buying from unverified suppliers — peptide purity varies enormously. Look for suppliers with third-party HPLC and mass spectrometry certificates of analysis (COAs) for every batch.

Tracking Your Progress

Without tracking, it is very difficult to know whether a peptide is working. Before starting any protocol, establish a baseline by noting the following:

→Pain / discomfort score — rate daily on 1–10. Photograph any visible swelling.
→Range of motion — note any stiffness or limitations before starting.
→Sleep quality — subjective 1–10 or use a wearable tracker.
→Energy & mood — simple daily note is sufficient.
→Body weight & composition — weekly weigh-in if relevant to your goal.

When you add bacteriostatic water to a peptide vial you create a solution with a known concentration. You then draw the volume that gives you your target dose using a U-100 insulin syringe — where 1 ml = 100 units.

Formula
Units to draw = (Dose mcg ÷ Vial mg × Water ml) ÷ 10

Quick-Reference Rules

Using 2ml of water per vial is the simplest baseline — it gives you clean, easy-to-remember numbers on a U-100 syringe. Here are the key rules.

5mg vial + 2ml water
250 mcg dose5 units
500 mcg dose10 units
1mg (1000 mcg) dose20 units
10mg vial + 2ml water
250 mcg dose2.5 units
500 mcg dose5 units
1mg (1000 mcg) dose10 units
2mg (2000 mcg) dose20 units
💡 The golden rule: With 2ml water, a 5mg vial = 500mcg per 10 units. If you remember nothing else, remember that.
📏 How to read the syringe: A U-100 insulin syringe has 100 small lines per ml. Each line = 1 unit = 0.01ml. Most peptide doses fall between 2 and 20 units.

Subcutaneous (SC) injection places the peptide in the fatty tissue just below the skin — this gives predictable absorption and is easy to self-administer.

1
Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water. Dry completely.
2
Swab the vial septum with an alcohol wipe. Let it air-dry for 10 seconds.
3
Draw air equal to your dose into the syringe, inject air into the vial (prevents vacuum), then invert and draw your dose.
4
Choose a site — lower abdomen (2–3 inches from navel), outer thigh, or flank. Rotate sites each injection.
5
Pinch the skin lightly. Insert at 45–90° depending on tissue depth. For lean individuals, 45° is safer.
6
Inject slowly — push the plunger steadily over 5–10 seconds. Fast injection increases local irritation.
7
Withdraw at the same angle. Apply gentle pressure with a swab — do not rub.
8
Dispose of the needle safely in a sharps container. Never re-use needles.
⚠️ Never inject into a vein, muscle, or into skin that is red, swollen, or infected. If you experience persistent pain, swelling, or feel unwell after injection, seek medical advice.
